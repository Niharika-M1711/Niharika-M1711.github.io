Model Comparison Report

TEC Forecasting: Which Model Wins?

Comparing three machine learning approaches for forecasting Total Electron Content (TEC) over India — evaluated on real 2025 data

The Models

Three Approaches Compared

ConvLSTM
Convolutional LSTM Encoder–Decoder
A deep learning model that learns spatial patterns (convolution) and time patterns (LSTM) together — sees the whole map evolving day by day.
Trained on: full year 2024 — quiet, minor, moderate, and severe days together
Random Forest
RF — ensemble of decision trees
Averages predictions from many decision trees. Treats each location mostly independently; no explicit sense of the spatial map.
Trained on: quiet days only
XGBoost
XGB — gradient boosted trees
Builds trees sequentially, each one correcting the previous one's errors. Very strong at fitting smooth, low-variability patterns.
Trained on: quiet days only

Verdict
Recommended Model
ConvLSTM
ConvLSTM is competitive with XGBoost and consistently beats Random Forest, despite having no special advantage on these quiet-day test cases. More importantly, it is the only model validated across the full range of conditions — quiet, minor, moderate, and severe storms alike — achieving 91.8% accuracy (R²) over the entire year. RF and XGBoost have never been tested beyond quiet days, so their real-world reliability during storms remains unknown.
Caveat: On quiet days specifically, XGBoost is narrowly more accurate. If the goal is only calm-condition forecasting, XGBoost is a fair alternative — but for general-purpose, year-round forecasting, ConvLSTM is the safer choice.
Additional Notes

Other Findings

  • Separate ConvLSTM models per storm category (quiet-only, moderate-only, severe-only) were attempted, to mirror RF/XGB's approach.
  • 2024 had zero continuous 8-day windows of Moderate or Severe activity — real storms typically last only 1–3 days before subsiding — so category-specific training was not possible for those two categories.
  • Where it was possible (Quiet: 77 days, Minor: 19 days), the resulting models badly overfit and generalized poorly — confirming that ConvLSTM needs the full year of continuous data to perform well, unlike RF/XGB.
